**Reading of the sources.** The sources describe the relationship in two ways. News reports from 2024 describe a single new party, "not a technical bloc". IDI and JPost describe a partial merger in which the two parties keep their separate institutions and corporations. The official CEC filing settles the 2026 structure: it is a list submitted by **two registered parties**, "הדמוקרטים מיסודה של תנועת העבודה" and "מרצ". Under §2.3 (v1.1), Meretz is therefore a predecessor through the joint-list clause. Labor is a predecessor through the merger and rename, documented by the sources in row 1. Under v1.1, achievements must be credited to the member party by name: Labor (now "הדמוקרטים מיסודה של תנועת העבודה") or Meretz.

## Open issues

- **Labor → The Democrats: legal mechanism.** Every approved source agrees that The Democrats continue Labor (a merger, with Labor's name changed). None of them is a Party Registrar record. The Registrar's open dataset on data.gov.il, last modified 2026-10-17, still lists "מפלגת העבודה הישראלית" (59-900001-5) and has no "הדמוקרטים מיסודה של תנועת העבודה". That dataset seems stale (it lists only 170 parties), and data.gov.il isn't an approved host. The CEC filing uses the new name, so the two official sources differ on the current registered name. The reviewer should decide whether the Knesset protocol, IDI and 4 independent news publishers are enough for §2.3, or whether a Registrar or Reshumot (ילקוט הפרסומים) notice of the rename is needed. The Knesset legal adviser's statement that Labor filed the 25th-Knesset list confirms that faction #1100 is Labor's.
- **Labor 1992 = Labor 2022.** The Jordan treaty is credited through the 13th-Knesset faction "מפלגת העבודה הישראלית" (#571). That it is the same party as the 25th-Knesset "העבודה" (#1100) is not seriously in doubt. Still, the only document found that says so (registration 59-900001-5) is on data.gov.il, which isn't an approved host.
- **CEC host.** The CEC now publishes candidate lists on www.gov.il (https://www.gov.il/he/pages/candidates-lists-26). `sources.ts` maps `cec` only to bechirot.gov.il. Consider adding `www.gov.il` to `cec` hosts (a data fix) so the leader source can be labelled "Central Elections Committee". gov.il pages return 403 to WebFetch and curl and had to be opened in a browser.
- **Overlap rule.** Labor and Meretz sat in the 24th Knesset during the same days. §2.4 doesn't say whether a joint list's predecessors' overlapping periods are summed or counted once by calendar day. It doesn't affect this party, since Labor alone has 2,021 days.
- **Scope of §3.1 for Labor and its earlier predecessors.** Labor-led governments signed several instruments establishing diplomatic relations with states, besides the Jordan treaty. Examples: the Holy See Fundamental Agreement (1993-12-30, Rabin), which provides for diplomatic relations, though whether the Holy See counts as a "state" for §3.1 needs a ruling; Mauritania (1999-10-28, Barak, One Israel joint list); and many 1992–96 establishments of relations. Mapai and the Alignment (המערך) era (e.g. West Germany, 1965) would count only if a source documents Mapai → Labor continuity (the 1968 merger). None of these were verified on approved hosts in this pass. §3.1 says to show all of them, so a methodology decision is needed: do bilateral communiqués establishing diplomatic relations count as "agreements", and how far back does the predecessor chain go? Then a dedicated pass is needed.
- **Reforms and legislation.** There were no qualifying items in pass 1. The evaluation-body test (before/after values from the State Comptroller, BoI, CBS, Knesset RIC, IDI or doi.org) wasn't met for any candidate checked. A dedicated pass could look at Labor and Meretz ministers in the 36th government (2021–22: Michaeli, Transport; Bar-Lev, Public Security; Horowitz, Health; Zandberg, Environmental Protection; Frej, Regional Cooperation) and at Labor MKs' private laws in the 20th–24th Knessets, starting from Knesset RIC reports. Meretz items must be credited to Meretz by name (v1.1).
