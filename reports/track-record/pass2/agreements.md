# Pass 2 — agreements (methodology v1.2 §3.1)

Snapshot 2026-10-18 · Methodology v1.2 · Researcher: automated pass 2 (to be independently verified)

**Scope.** Under v1.2 §3.1, a qualifying agreement is any signed treaty or agreement between Israel and another state that ends a state of war, or establishes or restores diplomatic relations. It is credited only to the party of the prime minister leading the coalition at signing, or to that party's documented predecessor (§2.3). "Signed" means a signed text exists: a treaty, a joint communiqué or declaration signed by both sides, or an exchange of letters or notes.

**How this pass worked.** Every URL below was opened on 2026-10-18 and checked against the row it supports. The methods were WebFetch, curl, or the built-in browser for www.gov.il pages, which return HTTP 403 to fetch tools. No archive copies were requested from web.archive.org.

**PM and party records.** Each PM's term and Knesset faction come from Knesset open data (KNS_PersonToPosition): PositionID 45 is prime minister and PositionID 54 is faction membership. Each record was opened at `https://knesset.gov.il/Odata/ParliamentInfo.svc/KNS_PersonToPosition(<id>)?$format=json`.

**Completeness.** Coverage is not complete. Agreements from before about 1995 are poorly covered by approved online sources, so many establishments of relations from the 1980s and 1990s could not be checked. They are listed in Open issues as leads. Leaving one out is not a finding that no signed text exists.

## Continuity: Likud 1977 → today

**Finding: documented (high).** The Israel Democracy Institute (idi, on the closed list as an evaluation body) says the following on its Likud page (idi \| https://www.idi.org.il/policy/parties-and-elections/parties/likud/ \| 2026-10-18):
- Origin: "הליכוד החל את דרכו כרשימה משותפת שהוקמה בשנת 1973 … וכללה את חרות, את המפלגה הליברלית, את המרכז החופשי, את הרשימה הממלכתית ואת התנועה למען ארץ ישראל השלמה". (Likud began as a joint list founded in 1973 that included Herut, the Liberal Party and others.)
- Merger: "בשנת 1988 התמזגו המפלגות לכדי מפלגה אחת בשם הליכוד". (In 1988 the parties merged into one party named Likud.)
- Begin: "הליכוד, בראשותו של מנחם בגין, הגיע לראשונה לשלטון בשנת 1977". (Likud, led by Menachem Begin, first came to power in 1977.)
- Shamir: "יצחק שמיר כיהן כראש ממשלה מטעם הליכוד בשנים 1992-1986". (Yitzhak Shamir served as prime minister for Likud in 1986–1992.)
- Later PMs: the page names Netanyahu (1996–99, 2009–21, and the 37th government from late 2022) and Sharon (2001–05). It says Sharon left in late 2005 to found Kadima.

**Reading under §2.3.** The 1977 Likud was a joint list. Its member parties, including Begin's Herut, merged into a single party named Likud in 1988. That is "a merger in which B joined A". IDI presents today's Likud as that party, with only the 2005 Kadima split along the way.

**Supporting record.** Knesset open data shows Begin in the faction "הליכוד" in the 9th Knesset (record 27127, faction 397), and Netanyahu in "הליכוד" in the 25th Knesset (record 30204, faction 1096). A matching name is not itself a §2.3 ground, so it is supporting evidence only.

**Caveats:**
- Sharon's Knesset faction changed from "הליכוד" to "אחריות לאומית" on 2005-11-23 (record 28518) and then to Kadima. Anything signed after 2005-11-23 under Sharon is **not** Likud's. None was found.
- Netanyahu's factions in 1996–99 (#637 "ליכוד - גשר - צומת") and 2013–14 (#804 "הליכוד - ישראל ביתנו") were joint lists. Under v1.1, credit goes to the member party, Likud, by name.

## Open issues

1. **Somaliland (likud-somaliland-2025).** Contributor decision: does Somaliland count as "another state" under §3.1? The facts and signing are well sourced (ToI and JPost). Only Israel is reported to recognize it.
2. **Same question for Kosovo and the Holy See.** Kosovo (likud-kosovo-2021) is not a UN member and its recognition is contested. The Holy See (labor-holy-see-1993) is a sovereign entity, not a UN member state. The contributor should apply one consistent rule to Somaliland, Kosovo and the Holy See. Under §8, if the wording of §3.1 needs clarifying, that is a methodology change.
3. **Israel–Lebanon agreement of 17 May 1983 (Begin, Likud).** It was signed and declared that "the state of war between Lebanon and Israel has ended and no longer exists" (jpost \| https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/article-900727 \| 2026-10-18, dated 2026-06-28). The same article says "the agreement never took effect". Ynetnews says it "was formally annulled about a year and a half later" (ynet \| https://www.ynetnews.com/opinions-analysis/article/ryjo54x6wx \| 2026-10-18, datePublished 2026-04-17). Begin was PM then: government 19, 1981-08-05 to 1983-10-10 (knesset \| https://knesset.gov.il/Odata/ParliamentInfo.svc/KNS_PersonToPosition(4066)?$format=json \| 2026-10-18). Read literally, §3.1 covers a signed treaty that ends a state of war. Contributor decision: does an agreement that never entered into force and was later annulled qualify? Neither the treaty text nor an official source for it was found.
4. **Washington Declaration, Israel–Jordan (1994-07-25, Rabin, Labor).** JPost: Rabin and King Hussein "signed the Washington Declaration, officially ending the state of enmity between Israel and Jordan" (jpost \| https://www.jpost.com/history/article-862095 \| 2026-10-18, dated 2025-07-26).
